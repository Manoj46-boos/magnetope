## Decisions that differ from, or interpret, the PRD

1. **Plane-locked 3D.** Physics bodies move in the X/Y plane; the look is full 3D with a long lens. The PRD's cursor-relative mouse aim, left-half touch stick and snap cone all imply a 2D control plane, and it keeps touch play precise.
2. **Retry.** The PRD lists Space and any click as "retry after a fall", but those are also the flip inputs. R restarts at any time, the fall card has a Retry button, and a fall otherwise lets you carry on from where you landed.
3. **Music is synthesised, not Opus stems.** Same layering rules and one key per biome, zero download.
4. **Bloom, vignette and grading are a real post pass now** (session 6). The grade tables are generated from parameters, not authored in Blender as the PRD describes, so they are tuned by eye in `render/grade.ts`.
5. **The planner bot is a search planner, not the PPO agent.** It answers "is this completable and how forgiving is each hop"; the PPO difficulty bots are a later tool.
6. **Font:** Fredoka (SIL Open Font License 1.1), approved by the developer and self-hosted since session 7. `public/fonts/fredoka-latin.woff2` is a 29 KB Latin and Latin-Extended subset, weights 400 to 700, taken from Google's fonts repository, with its licence beside it. System fonts remain the fallback while it loads.
7. **Poki SDK:** adapter written to the documented API. `measure(category, what, action)` was checked against Poki's Game Events guide in session 7; the other calls match developers.poki.com/guide/sdk-html5.
8. **The second seven are planner-verified drafts.** They are solvable but unplayed. `npm run design -- <id> --kinds=<prop>` solves a level, counts the grabs on a signature prop and tests whether the level still solves without it. Scrapyard 07 (loose girders) and Metropolis 12 (cladding) now require their prop. Scrapyard 09 (drawer cabinets) and Suburbia 08 (trampolines) do not: steel walls can be crawled, and the planner never plans bounces, so a bounce prop cannot be proven required by the bot.
9. **Scripted hazards are on the run clock, not wall time.** Fair and learnable, at the cost of the world standing still until the first input. The tutorial level has no hazards, so the first minute is unaffected.
10. **Par ghosts on hazard levels run at the planner's pace.** Padding a ghost with rests would drift it off the moving platforms. The par time still decides the third star; hand-recorded par ghosts replace these later.
11. **Biome chunking is not needed yet.** All level data is a few kilobytes; the bytes are in code and there are no downloaded meshes or stems to stream. Chunking returns when real art assets do.
12. **Star gates are the PRD numbers** (15, 34, 57, 80) now that every biome ships fourteen levels.
13. **Twists are gates, not just aids, from the Playground on.** Earlier biomes keep ledges every 5 to 8 m, so a good player can skip a twist; that is a risky shortcut, as the PRD invites. In the ten gated levels the fixed ledges sit at least one flip plus a snap apart (about 25 m at full gravity, 36 to 44 m in the Stratosphere). Other Playground and Stratosphere levels keep a staircase of fixed ledges on purpose, for the breather and the shortcut.
14. **Non-magnetic scenery is used to keep twists honest.** Satellites are foil-wrapped and hold no magnet, swing brackets are timber, walls are blocks or insulation: a player can bounce off them but cannot use them as stepping stones.
15. **The finale moved.** The Core and the ending now belong to `stratosphere-14`; level 7 is Cold Front. The film has not been heard or watched in a real play-through, only checked in the browser pane at several moments.
16. **Friend ghosts travel as text, not through Poki's AUDS.** The AUDS ghost exchange named in the PRD could not be verified against public documentation, so nothing was written against it. Codes keep the feature CSP-safe and offline. The PRD's re-simulation check is impossible because a ghost holds positions, not inputs; the plausibility check in `sim/ghost-vet.ts` stands in for it and cannot stop a hand-drawn but physically plausible route.
17. **Fall-rate targets are design intent, not measurement.** There is no PPO bot yet, so the curve is authored, not verified.
18. **The driven wheel is physical, not scripted, and only Twin Wheels uses it.** The other wheel levels keep the scripted wheel (reliable timing for the first meeting). The wheel's hub is 600 kg on purpose: a light hub against six welded spokes made Rapier's solver explode, a heavy one is stable. Cabins hang 1.7 m under the tips from a single pivot, so they tilt when MAGNO stands off-centre; heavier cabins would tilt less but slow the wheel. The numbers are untested by a human.

## Findings worth knowing

- **A fast arrival is inside the capture gap before it touches.** At 16 m/s MAGNO covers 0.27 m a tick, so a ferrous surface captured him before the impact was ever detected. Boing is therefore decided at capture as well as at impact.
- **A board hinged at its end sags under its own weight.** The hinge motor now aims at the rest angle plus that sag, which leaves a see-saw (hinged at its middle) untouched.
- **A restored snapshot used to lose broken glass for good**: the surface table was pruned on restore. It now keeps a catalogue of every surface the level was built with.
- **The diving board rewards timing, not just strength.** A flip released as the board rebounds adds the board's own speed (about 3 m of extra height); released while it plunges it costs 13 m.

- **Reach, with the stick held up as any player does.** One full-charge flip carries MAGNO 20.8 m at full gravity and a flip plus snap catches a ledge 21.4 m up. At gravity x0.62 that is 32 m and at x0.5 it is 39 m (`npm run reach -- 1 0.62 0.5`). The planner finds hops a little beyond that, so the gating rule adds a margin.
- **Air steering is scaled for light air.** The PRD's 2.4 m/s^2 of steering is a quarter of gravity at x1 but half of it at x0.5, which turned holding up into flying. Sideways steering stays at full strength (a gust must always be fightable); the upward share is now a fixed fraction of gravity, so every level keeps "steering, not flying".
- **Low gravity needs lighter wind.** Flights last three times longer at x0.5, so drift grows nine-fold for the same gust. Stratosphere gusts are 1.4 to 2.2 m/s^2, where Metropolis uses up to 3.6.
- **Rapier motors are acceleration-based by default.** The see-saw spring looked rigid until the joint motor was switched to force-based units (N m per radian). A hinged see-saw in real units tips under MAGNO and eases back; the same joint in default units barely moves.
- **A hinge joint and locked axes fight each other** in Rapier. Hung props leave their axes free; the joint already holds them to the plane.
- **The snap spring's counter-gravity now follows the level's gravity**, not the global constant. At x0.42 the old value lifted MAGNO past his target.
- **The planner's slip rate is a difficulty index, not a fall rate.** It rises with tighter hops, but it does not match the authored `fallRateTarget` numbers. Real fall rates need playtests or the PPO bots.

- **A float target makes a stray NaN visible.** One material produced NaN and the bloom smeared it into a black block around MAGNO. The bright pass now discards NaN and clamps spikes; `tests/post-stack.test.ts` reproduces the fault with a deliberately broken material.
- **Changing the quality tier now changes the pixel ratio.** The renderer used to keep the ratio computed for the tier it booted with, so stepping a tier down never reduced the resolution cost.

- **Stored routes are needle-precise (session 11).** A replay-based human-like bot (timing slips, aim error, re-planning) was built and dropped. Shifting MAGNO 1 cm on a ledge flips the outcome of some stored hops (the tutorial's last hop, scrapyard-03 hop 3), and adding any closed-loop steering makes a perfect-hands replay fail everywhere. So replayed routes cannot give trustworthy fall rates; only real players or a closed-loop learner (the PRD's PPO agent) can. Cold-load and render budgets, by contrast, measure exactly: draw calls peak at 91 of 120, triangles at 39k of 350k.
