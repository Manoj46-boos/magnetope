# The performance budget

Generated by `npm run docs` from the budget constants the audits and suites actually gate against, so the published table cannot drift from the enforced one. The build audit (`check-budget.mjs`) fails on any class over its line; the load gate owns the cold-load numbers; the browser battery owns the frame laws; the suite re-renders this file and compares it byte for byte.

## The payload (R-PE-01)

The whole game fits 4.0 MB gzipped against Poki's 5 MB hard limit, and inside that total every asset class carries its own line, so no class can quietly spend another's headroom. The WebAssembly is the engine's own and may grow by at most a quarter of its shipped size (R-PE-07), and it must arrive as a file the browser compiles while it downloads — the audit asserts the streaming path exists in the bundle.

## The frame (R-PE-02)

A 60 fps frame is 16.67 ms. The simulation's step is budgeted at p99 3.5 ms and the whole animation layer at p99 1.5 ms (measured on the reference phone at 4x the development machine); whatever remains is the render and composite share, documented per tier below. The watchdog may spend it, never borrow against it.

| Tier | Promised frame | Effect ceilings | Render + composite share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| high | 60 fps (16.7 ms) | 900 particles / 18 streaks | 11.7 ms |
| mid | 60 fps (16.7 ms) | 500 particles / 12 streaks | 11.7 ms |
| low | 30 fps (33.3 ms) | 260 particles / 6 streaks | 28.3 ms |

## The step's own discipline (R-PE-09)

One simulation tick goes straight to the engine's pipeline, because the compatibility wrapper rebuilds its handle maps after every step — about 20 KB a tick once the world is awake, the single largest allocation the game ever made. What remains is the contact probe's own cost: the engine hands out a fresh wrapper and a projection for every collider near the capsule, and the step's contact records are built fresh because the grounding state keeps references to them. The quietest of three rounds measures 15-21 KB a tick on the busiest levels, and the soak fails above 24 KB: the ceiling exists to catch the wrapper coming back, not to bless the floor.

## The laws of the running game

- **The watchdog steps down only at boundaries (R-PE-08)** — a frame-time average over budget for a full 4 s arms the step down, which then fires only while MAGNO is perched and quiet, never in flight and never mid-charge, and never below low. Every decision is logged (`watchdog` in the event vocabulary) with the frame rate that made it.
- **No long task in play (R-PE-12)** — after the first input, any main-thread task over 50 ms fails the battery where the harness can falsify it: the observer reads the ledger while the game really plays, and on a machine whose frames themselves sit under the line the law is absolute; on software rendering, where every heavy frame is long by itself, the ledger instead fails any task four times the frame it lives in — a collection storm or a rebuild stall cannot hide behind a slow renderer either way.
- **Permanence of instantaneity (R-PE-13)** — a retry rebuilds the level inside 50 ms (the promise to players is 400), and the road from a clear card to the next level's first drawn frame, card and all, fits 1 s with no loading screen in between, ever.
- **Memory holds its shape (R-PE-10)** — ten minutes on the busiest level without growth or a NaN, and three full load/unload walks of all seventy levels inside two percent of the heap they started from. The caches are capped where they live: 900 particles and 18 wind streaks at the top tier, 200 queued telemetry records.
- **Tier targets (R-PE-03)** — tier-high holds 60 fps on desktop and on flagship phones (the probe matrix says so), tier-mid 60 on a 2020 mid-range profile, and tier-low at least 30 on the 2021 budget-Android profile the PRD names (Mali-G52 class). The absolute floors are real-silicon promises, owned by the device matrix and the host's own device report; the software-rendered battery holds what any machine can falsify — every cheaper profile must genuinely buy frames, tier-low at least twice the frames of tier-high on the same machine. Dynamic resolution works within the tier before any downgrade is considered.
- **The device report is the oracle (R-PE-11)** — after the first player-fit test, Poki's own device report becomes the tier map's authority: `npm run tiermap` reads a week's device distribution and names the probes and lines to recalibrate. Until then the first-frame probe matrix is the map, and the boot record carries the tier it chose.
