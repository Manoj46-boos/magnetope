# The audio bible

Generated by `npm run docs` from the score's own tables — the motif and cells in `src/audio/score.ts`, the keys and stems in `src/audio/mix.ts`, the stinger grammar in `src/audio/stingers.ts` and the mix hierarchy in `src/audio/engine.ts`. The suite re-renders this file and compares it byte for byte, so a hand edit or a table change without a re-render fails the build (R-AU-07).

Zero bytes ship and zero bytes are fetched: every voice is synthesized from the biome’s single key, so any combination is consonant and the whole identity weighs nothing (the payload law, and the Stocco doctrine besides — the world is the orchestra).

## The motif (R-AU-01)

Five scale degrees, one rising shape, the fifth wrapping to the octave: `•0 •1 •2 •3 •5` as indices into the biome’s scale. Whatever key the game is in, the same contour spells the game’s name — and it is present at every payoff:

- **The boot hum** — the melody’s first cell is the motif statement, so the identity is the first thing the score says, inside three seconds of the first update.
- **The clear card** — the clear stinger quotes all five notes and resolves on the octave.
- **The biome reveal** — the biome call states the shape slowly, in the new biome’s own spelling.
- **The ending resolve** — the credits sign the same name the boot hummed.

The hummability verdict (four of five testers reproduce it after one session) is the playtest protocol’s leg, not CI’s — the notes, their presence and their order are what the suites hold.

## The stems and the tiers (R-AU-02)

Four stems — melody, bass, percussion, air — each rotating its own cells:

| Stem | Cells in the rotation |
| --- | --- |
| mel | 4 |
| bass | 3 |
| perc | 3 |
| air | 2 |

Three tiers the run earns (Prunty): **rest** — idle on a ledge, the motif statements and the air alone; **play** — traversal, the bass walks and the junk taps; **flight** — a launch or a snap chain, everything open. The crossfade law is half a second; the score changes hands in a tenth of it. A cell may return but never as itself: the melody alternates its octave by the pass, the air turns its inversion, the percussion walks its materials — and the take jitter does the rest (R-AU-10).

## The junk orchestra (R-AU-04)

The score’s percussion is the physics’ own instrument table: every hit is one of the biome’s material voices, struck by the same recipe a collision plays, routed through the score’s own stem. The fridge doors, locker clanks and girder rings that catch MAGNO are the drum kit — one table for score and physics, and no sample is ever loaded, anywhere (the zero-bytes law).

## The stinger grammar (R-AU-05)

One musical word per verdict, each short enough to be felt and never stacked — the table is the grammar:

| Word | The moment that owns it | The voice | Length |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| clear | the summit — the clear card is coming | playFanfare | ≤ 1.50 s |
| star-stamp | each earned star lands on the clear card | playStarStamp | ≤ 0.26 s |
| par-beat | the last star of a clear that beat par | playParCall | ≤ 0.30 s |
| ghost-set | the run recorded as the level’s own ghost | playGhostSet | ≤ 0.50 s |
| fall-verdict | the fall card, for the small grades — the spectacular ones keep the stage for MAGNO’s deadpan | playFallTopper | ≤ 0.35 s |
| biome-enter | the biome card — the motif, re-harmonized | playBiomeCall | ≤ 1.40 s |

The ceremony law: the star stamps answer one another — the card's own rhythm spaces them — and the par coda and ghost toast answer the last stamp on the audio clock. The spectacular falls keep the stage for MAGNO’s own deadpan: the silence is the sting (the comedy law). Every word side-chains the bed -3 dB for its whole length, and the chirp bus holds its tongue around it (R-AU-06).

## The mix hierarchy (R-AU-07)

Katz’ published discipline — one table, the law the graph obeys:

| Bus | Target | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| sfx | -12 dB | the world: material voices, launches, the junk struck |
| amb | -24 dB | the room: biome ambience, wind, distant life |
| music | -18 dB | the score: four stems on one bed, ducked by ads and stingers |
| ui | -20 dB | the verdicts: stingers, stamps, ticks, the cards |
| chirp | -24 dB | MAGNO's twelve words, six under the bed and quiet under stingers |

- **Ads** — the bed ducks -6 dB (to a floor of -24 dB) over 0.12 s around any break, breathing out before the ad so no music tail crosses it (R-AU-09).
- **Stingers** — side-chain the bed -3 dB over 0.08 s, for the word's whole length, and never stack.
- **Monitoring** — the master's own gain is 0.85, the true-peak ceiling -2 dBTP; the loudness law itself is −14 LUFS integrated with per-biome parity inside 1 LU, measured offline on real renders (R-AU-08).

## Silence is composed (R-AU-12)

The Stratosphere teaches its thin air with the score’s own quiet: `stratosphere-01` runs the air stem alone, so the ear learns the dynamics the ending’s one through-composed cue spends — from the seat, the cues are the score, and the bed stands down. Global mute stays fully playable: the visual juice carries the meaning (R-GF-11).

## The performance take (R-AU-10)

Devine’s law: every repeat-prone voice — the snap’s zip, the clank, the chirp, the junk’s hits — wanders inside ±10% pitch and decay and ±20 ms of micro-timing per take, within its identity: a chirp never leaves its quarter second, a melody never leaves its tune, a UI blip never leaves its class peak. Three identical voices inside two seconds is machine-gunning, and the suites hold the line.
